# G71 Resolution — Product-Sphere Family and Uniqueness

**Date:** 2026-06-21

**Verdict:** The current potential does not select \(S^3\times S^6\)

**Evidence status:** [VERIFIED-SYNTHETIC] for the shared-model scan;
[NEEDS-REAL-DATA] for physical compactification uniqueness

## Verified result

The existing H1 scan applies the same one-dimensional effective-potential
framework to

\[
S^a\times S^{9-a},\qquad a=1,\ldots,5,
\]

with \(\lambda=a/9\).

Within that shared model, every tested case has:

- an interior AdS minimum above \(\rho_*\);
- positive coordinate-space second derivative along the scanned trajectory;
- a proxy hierarchy \(m_{\rm mod}/m_{\rm KK}\) between 1.17% and 2.60%.

Therefore:

> The tested one-dimensional potential does not uniquely select
> \(S^3\times S^6\).

## Scope limitation

This is not a complete comparison of compactification geometries. H1 keeps
the following \(S^3\times S^6\) inputs fixed instead of re-deriving them for
each product:

- \(\rho_*=1.090\);
- Freund–Rubin flux normalization;
- Einstein-frame volume scaling;
- Casimir contribution;
- the relation between the two radii;
- the moduli kinetic metric.

Consequently, the scan demonstrates structural sensitivity to \(\lambda\),
not physical viability of every sphere product.

## What remains open

Any uniqueness of \(S^3\times S^6\) must come from an independently
demonstrated spectral or representation-theoretic selection mechanism, not
from the current potential alone.

For \(S^6\), the relevant candidate structure is:

\[
S^6\simeq G_2/SU(3),
\]

with a \(G_2\)-invariant nearly-Kähler \(SU(3)\)-structure and the embedding
chain

\[
SU(3)\subset G_2\subset Spin(7)\subset Spin(8).
\]

This does not yet prove physical selection. In particular, the project has
not shown that the triality-related modules \(8_v,8_s,8_c\):

1. all occur in one physical Dirac action;
2. correspond to three distinct twisting bundles;
3. possess nonzero indices with the same chirality sign.

## Required full test

A physical G71 comparison would need, for each product geometry:

1. independently derived flux and Casimir terms;
2. its own UV-selection radius \(\rho_*\);
3. the full two-modulus Einstein-frame potential;
4. the canonical moduli kinetic metric;
5. positivity of the complete covariant Hessian;
6. comparison to the lightest KK scale.

Until those calculations exist, the correct statement is:

> Scale stabilization is not unique in the shared model; spectral uniqueness
> of \(S^6\) remains a hypothesis.
