# C236 (Q4): the "fourth harmonic" as the diagonal-SU(2) singlet; singlets in scalar and C²-valued sectors

Question type: [x] descriptive  [ ] predictive  [ ] causal.

Estimand sentence (written before running): we compute the number of SU(2)_diag singlets (and their form) in the harmonic sectors of S³ = SU(2) of degree 0..3, for scalar functions and for C²-valued functions, comparing the diagonal acting on functions only (D = X + Y), with the spin included (Dtot = X + Y + S), and with the spin untouched (Dorb).
MCID / sampling: not applicable (exact linear algebra, deterministic). n, baseline_or_sigma, alpha, power, MDE, MCID: not applicable.

Claim: (1) the four degree-1 harmonics split 1 ⊕ 3 under SU(2)_diag, the singlet is a = ½ tr g; (2) each scalar sector (s,s), s = 0, ½, 1, 3/2, has exactly one diagonal singlet, equal to the SU(2) character r^n U_n(a/r); (3) C²-valued fields of degree 0..3 have no Dtot singlet and two Dorb singlets; (4) D0 has the C235 spectrum (in C227 normalisation) and commutes with X and K.

What this does NOT mean: (a) It does not say the "fourth harmonic" of Tom is this singlet; his definition is unknown. (d) Four degrees only; the general statement (one singlet per scalar sector, none in spinor sectors since j_L − k = ±½) is Peter–Weyl/Clebsch–Gordan, not proved by the four cases.
