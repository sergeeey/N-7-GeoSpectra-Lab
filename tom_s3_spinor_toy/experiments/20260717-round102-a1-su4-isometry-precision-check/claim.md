## Section 1 — Background and the specific concern

Gate G97 (cited in `preprint.tex:280-286`, `:420-424`, `:1595-1596`, and
reused as the single dispositive blocker by rounds 90-97, round93 Part D,
and `dolan-casimir-g2su3`'s own Round 48A self-correction) states: **"no
`SU(4)` subgroup [exists] in `Iso(S³×S⁶)=SO(4)×SO(7)`."**

**Elementary Lie-theory concern, worth checking directly before accepting
this at face value for A1's search:** `so(6)` is a well-known subalgebra
of `so(7)` — the isotropy (stabilizer) subalgebra of a point on
`S⁶⊂ℝ⁷` under the round metric's full `SO(7)` isometry action, a standard
fact for any sphere (`so(n-1)⊂so(n)` always). Since `so(6)≅su(4)` (the
classical `D₃=A₃` exceptional Lie-algebra isomorphism), this appears, on
its face, to show `su(4)` genuinely IS a subalgebra of `so(7)` — seemingly
contradicting G97's literal wording.

**This experiment's job:** verify the elementary Lie-theory fact directly
(tool-verified, not asserted), and then carefully determine whether this
constitutes a genuine reopening of gate G97, or whether — as is plausible
— the PHYSICALLY relevant question G97 was actually checking is narrower
than the literal wording suggests (e.g., whether an `SU(4)` compatible
with the SPECIFIC, already-fixed `SU(3)_c` embedding via the `G₂/SU(3)`
coset construction, and consistent with the coset-space-dimensional-

much harder, more constrained question than "does an abstract `so(6)`
subalgebra of `so(7)` exist somewhere."
