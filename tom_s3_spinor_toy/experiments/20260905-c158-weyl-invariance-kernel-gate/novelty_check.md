## Finding 1 (decisive): Steps 3-4 reconstruct the classical Pin-cover of the Weyl group

Ciubotaru, *Spin representations of Weyl groups and the Springer correspondence*
(arXiv:1006.3823, building on Schur 1911 for `S_n`, completed for other root
systems by Morris/Read/Stembridge ~1980):

> "The Weyl group `W` is a finite subgroup of `O(V)` and therefore, one can
> consider the double cover `W~` of `W` in `Pin(V)`... The group `W~` is
> generated by certain elements `s~_alpha` **of order 4**, `alpha in R^+`."

**This is EXACTLY C158 Steps 3-4's own finding**, independently rediscovered
from scratch: `S01`, `S12` (Pin lifts of the two simple reflections of
`W(A_2)`) each satisfy `S^2 != +-I` — a genuine order-4 element, matching
Ciubotaru's `s~_alpha` precisely. This project's entire "build Pin(m), lift
the reflections, find they have order 4" arc **is** the construction of the
classical `W~` — over 100 years old for the `S_n` case (`A_2` here IS an
`S_3=S_{2+1}` Weyl group), ~40 years old in general.

## Finding 2: the psi_+/psi_- swap under Pin lift is classical index-theory territory

Wang, *Chern-Connes Character for the Invariant Dirac Operator in Odd
Dimensions* (arXiv:math/0609063), citing Freed (Lefschetz fixed-point formula
for orientation-reversing involutions on spin manifolds):

> "Let `tau` be an orientation-reversing isometric involution. Assume there
> exists a self-adjoint lift `tau~` ... satisfying `tau~^2=1`, `D tau~ = -tau~
> D`. ... the `+1` and `-1` eigenspaces of `tau~` give a splitting of the
> spinor fields ... **and the Dirac operator interchanges** [them]."

This is the SAME structural phenomenon as C158's `S.psi_+ = -i*psi_-`
(a Pin lift of an orientation-reversing map exchanging two distinguished
spinor subspaces, tied to the Dirac operator) — a fixture of Atiyah-Singer
/ Atiyah-Bott-Shapiro-style Pin-cobordism index theory since the 1970s-80s.
Not identical in setup (C158 is on a homogeneous space's isotropy module, not
a general spin manifold's involution), but the SAME mechanism.
